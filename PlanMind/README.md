# PlanMind

Private shared planner for the owner and the partner, part of MindSuite. Intended URL: `https://seancheren.com/PlanMind/`.

The browser interface and native Tauri shells share one HTTPS application. Only the existing configured `alex` and `sam` seancheren.com accounts can access its API. No registration or public sharing is added. Design and contract: [docs/ux/IMPLEMENTATION-SPEC.md](docs/ux/IMPLEMENTATION-SPEC.md) and the typed `packages/core/src/model.ts`; wireframes and the owner's notes are in `docs/ux/`.

- **Day**: Schedule (a planner day of 7a to 7a the next morning with half-hour creation slots and quarter-hour placement precision, in two pages behind an AM/PM switch on a desktop and a phone: AM is 7a to 9p, PM the night, 9p to 7a, at the same row height; the timeline fits the available height and scrolls only when its 8px quarter-hour floor requires more space; the page opens on AM, and an entry after midnight belongs to the evening's date), five typed Priorities per list, To-do as 13 ruled slots (open to-dos fill them top-down with a colored circle per owner; the empty ones are blank lines with a faint circle, and the first is the add field; a click on a blank line starts a to-do there, and in the Both view the line is cut in thirds like the schedule: the left third is the left lane's to-do, the middle third one linked to-do for both, the right third the right lane's, with the same hover ghost; Week and Month To-do work the same), Notes per list. Today's To-do also lists the open to-dos of earlier days, oldest first, with a muted "from Mon Oct 26" chip (a view only: they stay on their own day and no other day shows them). Ticking one there makes it done on today (`doneOn`, the owner 2026-11-02): it is checked in today's done pile, still with its chip, while its own day shows it unchecked with a small "→ Tue 3" chip and a circle that does nothing; it is no longer carried, and unchecking it brings it back. A to-do ticked on its own day is done there, as before. The Schedule controls and phone tabs stay in place as the timeline scrolls. On a desktop, Notes stays below the timeline. A new entry fits in the tapped half hour when there is room (30 minutes, with quarter-hour precision to fit existing entries; a shorter gap cannot hold an appointment; the nearest free start when the pointer lands on occupied time; in the Both view the schedule is cut in thirds across its width: the left third is the left lane's entry, the middle third one linked entry for both, the right third the right lane's), moved by dragging it (a mouse or pen press and move; a finger press and hold, then drag; Alt+Up and Alt+Down; in the Both view with the two lanes also across its thirds, which makes it the owner's, both of ours or the partner's: the same drag, or Alt+Left and Alt+Right) and resized with the start and end handles in edit mode (Alt+Shift+Up and Alt+Shift+Down move the end without a pointer); an edge an entry shares with the next one (one ends where the other starts, in a lane they share) is one boundary: dragging it moves both, neither shorter than 30 minutes, and Enter saves both in one all-or-nothing `editEvents` write (the owner, 2026-11-09); while it is edited a small pill over its block says its span. Every item is edited where it stands (a to-do, priority, entry or list row turns into a field in its own place; Enter or leaving saves, Escape cancels): no window opens for editing, and editing an entry is only its text. Delete is a trash icon that turns red on the first press and deletes on the second (no text, no Cancel; the press is forgotten after 2.5 seconds).
- **CalMind events on the Day** (the owner, 2026-11-09): the schedule also draws CalMind's timed events, faint (translucent, a dashed edge, a stripe in the CalMind calendar's color), in their owner's lane, under the planner's own entries. They are not entries: they cannot be clicked, dragged or edited, a tap or hover goes through them to the slot beneath, and an entry may be put on top of one (overlap checks ignore them). Overlapping CalMind events share their lane's width side by side (core `calmind.ts`). An event on both people's calendars (same planner day, start, length and words, case and spacing aside) is one piece across both lanes in the Both color, when both lanes are on the page side by side; beside other pieces it keeps to the inner side of each lane (the owner, 2026-11-09). The server reads them read-only from CalMind's own store on this host (`server/lib/planner_calmind.php`: `records-<user>.json` in `/home/protected/calmind-prod/data`, the test instance's `/home/protected/calmind/data`, `PLANMIND_CALMIND_DIR` locally; `ENC1:` under its `.datakey`): the viewer's own calendars whole, the other person's only the calendars they share with the viewer in CalMind (a mutual partnership; their per-partner selection or else the flat list). Repeats expand as CalMind's do; an event at 1a is on the evening before's night page; one that crosses 7a is cut into a piece per planner day. All-day events are a read-only strip above the timeline (below). The Both color is each person's own: an account preference (`planmindBothColor`, action `setBothColor`), the household `settings.bothColor` being the default until someone picks. The page asks for the open day, three days before and four weeks after, again every minute (`GET ?calmind=1&from=&to=`); a failed read keeps what it has. **All-day strip** (#171, the owner 2026-11-09): CalMind's all-day events (an event with no valid time) show in a compact strip between the lane names and the timeline, outside the timed grid so it never moves a card (`ScheduleSection.alldayHtml`, core `alldayCells` and `alldayStrip`). It has one row per lane at most two, a chip in its owner's lane or one chip across both lanes (the same pairing and visibility rules as the timed cards), in the timed cards' look (opaque, the calendar's stripe and tint); a lane with more shows one fewer chip and a `+N` whose tooltip lists the rest. No element is drawn on a day with none. The strip is on both the AM and the PM page. A **CalMind** button beside the AM/PM switch hides or shows them (the strip with them); the choice is per account, kept beside the theme in the account library's prefs file (`planmindCalmind`, action `setCalmind`), not in the planner store.
- **Week**: Monday to Sunday. **Month**: 42-cell grid of the month's chores (the Chores below: owner-tinted chips in each day on a desktop, a bar for each chore and the month's list under the grid on a phone; no dots or counts for what is scheduled; Week A rows lightly highlighted, a hatched Saturday, a date number that opens the day), Priorities and To-do beside it (on a desktop the Priorities take up to two thirds of that column, each card as tall as its words and at least eight lines showing; the To-do list scrolls inside itself), Notes after the last day. Week and Month text belong to one list each; the Both view shows one block per lane. Week and Month To-do are ruled lines like the Day's (at least 13 for a Week, 8 for a Month; the add field is on the first blank one). A tap on a Month day opens that Day with a Back to month button in the top bar.
- **Chores**: the household chart (rules, one mark per touched turn, the A/B rhythm), not to-dos. Chores ignore the picker: both of us see every chore. On a desktop the Month calendar always carries them, and the Day's picker menu has a "Chores" switch (a house) that swaps the To-do section for the day's chores in the same box (the Schedule, Priorities and Notes keep following the lists; the Week has none); there is no page of its own on a desktop. On a phone the last tab of each bar (Day 4, Week 3, Month 5) is a Chores page that takes the planner over with that bar still at the bottom and the picker a grey locked house; the Month's Calendar tab also draws a bar per chore in its cells and the month's list under the grid. Day: today's chores (a weekly, Week A / B or monthly one wears a quiet cadence badge), Missed or Catch-up, the done pile; Week (phone): the daily grid and the days; Month: owner-tinted chips. A circle in the owner's color checks a turn, a tap on its title hands that turn to the next owner (the partner, the owner, Both); a missed turn can be sent to the catch-up Friday or let go. "The rhythm…" in the picker menu (Day and Month) edits the rules (a new one is for both of us) and says whether this week is A or B.
- **Lists and the picker**: the picker is the one user selector: a new item is for the base list, or for both of us under Both, with no For control. The partner and the owner are permanent lists (color editable, never deleted). Both is a view over those two, not a list. Custom lists (up to 12) are created in the picker's manager; a row tap shows that list alone, a box tick adds it on top (up to 2). Both never includes a custom list on its own. The selection is saved per person, applied at load only.
- **Lanes**: the signed-in person is the left lane. An item added for Both is one record with two owners: one cell across both lanes, or two half cells when its priority lines differ.
- **Account and settings**: the account is one menu (Settings, Sign out) in the desktop top bar and the phone drawer. Settings has the suite's four themes, saved per account through the account library (the prefs file CalMind reads). A button at the left of the desktop top bar hides the sidebar; the choice is saved per person.
- **Phone**: top bar (hamburger, date, round picker; on a Day opened from the Month, Back to month stands beside the hamburger until it is pressed; the menu also holds the Day schedule's AM/PM and CalMind choices), bottom tabs from `PHONE_TABS`, date sheets (calendar, Monday-to-Sunday list, month grid). Every Day tab fits one screen; the caps below make that true (13 to-dos fit the To-do tab at 375x812).
- **Caps** (`CAPS` in `model.ts`, enforced by the server): 500 characters for a schedule entry in a lane or linked (a block shows as many lines of it as its height holds, then an ellipsis; the owner, 2026-11-01), 18 per priority line, 36 per day to-do, 13 open day to-dos per list (the owner, 2026-10-31; on today the carried-over ones count too), 400 day-note characters. Week and Month have no phone caps beyond the data limits.

## Development

Requires Node 24, PHP 8.2+ with OpenSSL and mbstring, and sibling `seancheren-site` and `CoreMind` checkouts (in a worktree, point at them with `PLANMIND_TEST_SITE_LIB=<seancheren-site>/lib` and `COREMIND_REPO=<CoreMind>`). Apply the handoff integration patches before checking CoreMind copies.

```sh
npm ci
npm test              # typecheck, limits drift, vitest, PHP cases, atomic, deploy harness
npm run build
npx playwright install chromium
npm run test:e2e      # PLANMIND_E2E_PORT=5190 to run beside another checkout
npm run check:core
npm run dev           # PLANMIND_PORT=5210 for another port
```

The preview uses disposable accounts and data, never the real site's configuration. It prints its localhost address and fixture credentials. The production build contains only public assets/PHP entrypoints and protected application libraries.

## Architecture

`packages/core` holds the rules and view models: selection and lanes, colors, placement, schedule, priorities, limits, month grid. It has no DOM, clock or storage. `app/` is plumbing: `state.ts` (snapshot, drafts in memory, polling, the picker, sidebar and theme choices), `api.ts`, `colors.ts` (tint variables per theme), `shell/` (page, top bar, sidebar, account menu, settings), `components/` (the date sheet), `inplace/` (editing where an item stands: the open edit's state, the keys and the write path), `panels/` (`DayPanel` + `day/`, `WeekPanel`, `MonthPanel`, `week/`, `month/`, and `chores/`: the one chore row and `ChoresView`, which the shell draws at phone width instead of a panel while chores mode is on) and `picker/` (the list picker, the list manager and the rhythm editor). `server/public/PlanMind/api.php` is a thin entrypoint over `server/lib`: `planner.php` (store, snapshot validation, `mutate()` dispatcher), `planner_rules.php`, `planner_items.php`, `planner_lists.php`, `planner_chores.php`, `planner_migrate.php`, and the generated `limits.php` (`node tools/gen-limits.mjs`; never hand-edit). All of it stays flat in `server/lib` so the deploy audit passes unchanged. An open page notices a new release: `api.php` names the release that answered in `X-PlanMind-Release`, `release.ts` compares the stamps, and the page reloads at the next quiet moment (no focused field, no unsent words, no pointer down, no write in flight), putting the person back through `place.ts` (the URL hash for that one load, never storage). Requests wait 45 s (`REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS`: a deploy slows the host); the desktop error banner overlays the page header and moves nothing.

Storage is one AES-256-GCM file outside the web root, plaintext `v: 4` (`doneOn` on a done day to-do joined `v: 3`, and nothing else changed: a stored v3 file is converted in memory on read; chore rules, marks and the rhythm joined `v: 2`: a stored v2 file is converted in memory too, seeding the owner's 21 chore rules; the first write replaces either). A v1 file (PlanMind 0.1.0) with records is converted in memory on read (`person` becomes `owners`; the one shared week or month note becomes the same text in both lists' notes; titles and notes over today's caps are kept, never truncated), and the file is only replaced by the first successful write, after its untouched bytes are saved once as `tasks.v1.enc.bak` beside it (putting that file back as `tasks.enc` undoes the conversion). Every store version bump does the same: the first write that stores a newer version first keeps the old file's exact bytes once as `tasks.v<old>.enc.bak` (v1, v2 or v3), and refuses to write when a different backup is already there. After each write `tasks.version` (the number and a newline, readable by group `web`) names the version the file holds, so `deploy.sh` can refuse to point the site at a release that cannot read it; delete it with the backup when putting one back. A v1 file that does not decrypt, or is not exactly what 0.1.0 wrote, fails closed with 503, bytes untouched. Writes serialize through CoreMind's checked lock/atomic-file helpers, carry per-record revisions, and adds are idempotent by client id plus a creation fingerprint.

CoreMind is not a runtime dependency. Eight exact copies are listed in the suite's `tools/shared-files.json` (`npm run check:core` compares them): themes, atomic storage and its checks, Tauri build.rs, the heavy-build lock, the machine-env loader and its sample, and the tdtp wrapper. AgentSuite is installed through its normal managed installer.

## Privacy

Every API request checks authentication and the configured two-account allowlist. Writes require CSRF validation and use per-record revisions; the theme is the one write that is not planner data (the account library's prefs file, `setTheme`). Login attempts are rate-limited at this app's endpoint, ten failures per client address and fifteen minutes (the client is the right-most `X-Forwarded-For` entry, because `REMOTE_ADDR` there is the load balancer; the client writes that entry, so this is only a speed bump), plus caps that name no address: ten failures per submitted account name and a hundred for the whole app per hour, refused with the answer of a wrong password and failing closed ([docs/LOGIN-LIMITS.md](docs/LOGIN-LIMITS.md) has the numbers and the denial-of-service price); the existing site's other login endpoints retain their own policy. Responses are non-cacheable, content is rendered as text, and nothing private is written to localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB, the Cache API or a service worker (an e2e test asserts all four are empty).

Encryption is at rest, not end-to-end: the trusted server holds the key. Backups must preserve the protected data directory **and its `.key`**. Missing keys or corrupt ciphertext fail closed rather than becoming empty lists. The native remote page has no Tauri IPC permissions and navigation is limited to the private HTTPS app.

## Native and deployment

`native/` contains Tauri desktop/iOS source and icons. `tools/build-platforms.sh` uses the suite heavy-build lock and accepts `--windows`, `--mac`, `--ios`, and `--dry-run`. iOS installation is restricted to the explicit UDIDs of the phones listed in `PLANMIND_PHONES` in the untracked `.local/machine.env` (`IOS_PHONES` replaces the list for one run); it never installs onto every discovered phone, and a phone that is offline or locked is skipped with a warning and listed as owed (not a failed run). The GitHub native workflow is manual and produces a draft private release.

The macOS and iOS apps are built and installed on the Mac and both iPhones by every release (Windows installers only with `--windows` on a Windows host: `sh tools/build-platforms.sh --windows` in Git Bash checks the MSVC Rust host and the Visual Studio C++ Build Tools before building, removes old installers, then requires both the new msi and nsis exe; it does not install them). `tools/deploy.sh` (test by default; `--prod --yes-prod`; `--dry-run`; `--rollback`) audits `dist/` for account/data/config files, uploads a new release beside the live paths, lints every PHP file on the host, then flips one link:

- Deploy touches only three places (`$PUBLIC_BASE` is `/home/public`, or `/home/public/test` on the test instance): `$PUBLIC_BASE/PlanMind` (the serving symlink), `$PUBLIC_BASE/.planmind/` (public release directories `<stamp>/`, plus a stable `.htaccess` of exactly `Require all denied`, so direct URLs are refused) and `/home/protected/planmind[-test]/` (`releases/<stamp>/lib/`, the `previous` stamp file, the lock). Why: Apache answers 403 (`AH00037`) for any symlink whose target is under `/home/protected`, but serves a symlink that stays inside the docroot; so the public code lives under the docroot and only the lib stays protected. There is no `current` pointer.
- `$PUBLIC_BASE/PlanMind` is a symlink with the relative target `.planmind/<stamp>` (created last on a first install; a real directory or a foreign symlink there aborts, "move it aside"). The flip is one `ln -sfh` + `mv -fh`. `index.php`/`api.php` bind to their own lib by the name of the directory they run from (`basename(__DIR__)` must match `^\d{14}-\d+$`, lib = `/home/protected/planmind[-test]/releases/<stamp>/lib/bootstrap.php`), so a flip can never pair a new page with an old lib even while PHP still runs a cached old entrypoint. `PLANMIND_LIB_DIR` (local preview, e2e) wins; with neither resolving they answer 503 without naming a path. `api.php` sends `X-PlanMind-Release: <stamp>` on every response (not with `PLANMIND_LIB_DIR`).
- The host stamps each release with its own name (`release.txt`, written during the lint step, never part of the audited payload). Verification: page 200; unauthenticated `api.php` 401 whose `X-PlanMind-Release` equals this release's stamp (any other status is final); `release.txt` = stamp; `build.txt` = version; the direct `/.planmind/<stamp>/index.php` URL answers 403 or 404. One 150 s window (`VERIFY_WINDOW`) is shared by all these requests: a request with no answer waited 20 s (`CURL_WAIT`) and is asked again, and a value that is still the old release's (the `api.php` stamp while PHP's realpath cache lets go of the old entrypoint, a static file a slow host has not switched) is asked again once a second, every wait counted against the window (on 2026-11-02 the first PHP request of a new release took over 20 s three deploys in a row). That proves *which* release is served even when the version did not change. A failed verification rolls back automatically, but only while this run's release is still the one the link names; `previous` is restored to what it was before the failed deploy. A failed **first** install is taken offline again (link removed, both release directories discarded: back to "not installed"). `--rollback` re-points the link at `previous` (the old live release becomes `previous`) and verifies it by its own stamp the same way; it can never remove the only release, and it refuses a release whose `lib/store-version.txt` is missing or lower than the data's `tasks.version` (the build ships `store-version.txt`; it must equal the version `limits.php` reads and writes).
- `/home/protected/planmind[-test]/previous` is a plain file holding the previous stamp (written by a temporary file and one rename). The newest 5 releases are kept, counted over both trees (`.planmind/<stamp>` and `releases/<stamp>`); the live and previous releases are never pruned.
- One deploy at a time: prepare takes `/home/protected/planmind[-test]/.deploy.lock` (also taken by `--rollback`) and every way out (error, Ctrl-C, kill) sends an unlock. A lock older than 30 minutes is reported as stale, with the exact `ssh ... rm -rf` command to clear it; it is never broken automatically.
- Never touched: `/home/protected/planmind-data[-test]` (web-owned, provisioned separately), the site's `lib[-test]/`.
- Needs beforehand: the site's `lib[-test]/auth.php`, `config.php` and files `auth.php` requires; `/home/protected/planmind-data[-test]` owned by the SSH user and group `web`, mode 2770 (`mkdir -p DIR && chgrp web DIR && chmod 2770 DIR`). The prepare phase **fails** on both lanes when it is missing or not group-writable, printing those commands, and prints the host's `php -v`.
- Optional protected `calmind-both-calendar.json` in the instance's PlanMind data directory selects one calendar to span Both in the Day view and appear in either personal view. Use `{"owner":"alex","calendarId":"<CalMind calendar ID>"}` with group `web` and mode 0640. An absent or invalid file keeps the default placement. CalMind's sharing rules still govern access. Keep the production selection outside the release payload and source control.
- **Lanes (2026-11-02):** `dtp` is the fast lane: `tools/deploy.sh --quick` runs `check:core`, `npm test`, the build and the spot e2e (`planner.spec.ts`'s private API and cross-account poll, both projects); `tdtp` is `dtp.sh --full`, the same with the whole browser suite. Both fetch `origin/main` first and refuse (before anything is deployed) when origin is ahead or already has the version's tag. Right after the verified deploy they tag and push (a live version is never left untagged), then run `tools/build-platforms.sh` (`--web` skips it) and report what is still owed: a failed build, or a phone that was offline or locked. `tools/package.mjs` empties `dist/protected` before copying so a removed lib file can never ship.
- Host work is light: one `php -l` process lints every file, rsync sends with `--chmod=Dgo+rx,Fgo+r` (macOS's own rsync ignores it, so the host opens what the upload left closed), and every phase prints how long it took.
- Standalone release lanes report to the shared status page through CoreMind, with a heartbeat each minute and a final result for the web release and device rollout. A suite batch uses its parent status card.
